Tromsø is one of the easiest places in the world to see the northern lights. The city lies so far north that the auroral oval often sits right overhead, or just to the north, even on quiet nights. What usually stops you is not a lack of aurora but clouds and city lights. With a little planning you can see the aurora on foot from the island of Tromsøya, and when the coast is overcast it can pay to drive inland.

## Why Tromsø is so well placed

The aurora is strongest in a belt around the magnetic pole. Tromsø lies at about 69.6° north, but what counts is magnetic latitude, which is around 67° by our calculation with the AACGM-v2 coordinate model. NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center says the equatorward edge of the auroral oval sits at roughly 66° magnetic latitude when Kp is 0 and moves about 2° towards the equator for each step on the Kp scale. In other words, Tromsø is inside the zone where aurora is common even at low activity. Read more in [the Kp index](id:kp-index).

Aurora science has deep roots in Northern Norway; the story of the world's first aurora observatory is told in our article on [Alta and Finnmark](id:finnmark).

The season runs from late August to early April, according to Visit Tromsø. The polar night (Norwegian: mørketid), when the sun does not rise, lasts from about 26 November to 17 January (Store norske leksikon). See [best time of year](id:best-season) for what each month offers.

## Spots in town you can walk or bus to

The regional tourist board's guide to seeing the aurora on foot in Tromsø describes several places that are easy to reach without a car. A selection, from most to least light pollution:

- **The harbour and Nordjetéen:** The breakwater by the Tromsø Bridge (Tromsøbrua) looks towards the bridge and the Arctic Cathedral (Ishavskatedralen). City light is strong, so this works best when the aurora is bright.
- **Prestvannet:** The lake on top of Tromsøya, about 20 minutes from the centre. New housing and lit paths have made it brighter than before, but you get a lot of open sky. Never walk onto the ice unless you know it is safe.
- **Telegrafbukta:** A beach near the southern tip of the island, facing east, south and west. Easy to reach, but the city lies to the north, so faint aurora can fade against the glow.
- **Holmboevannene and Grønnåsen:** Small lakes and ski jumps in the woods on the northern and central parts of the island, with less light.
- **Fjellheisen and Storsteinen:** The cable car takes about four minutes up to Storsteinen, 421 metres above sea level, with a view over the whole city. Check opening hours; its website says the gondola runs until midnight. You can also climb the Sherpa Steps (Sherpatrappa), but in winter that takes ice cleats, a headlamp and a judgement of snow conditions.
- **Tromsdalen:** The valley behind the Arctic Cathedral quickly takes you away from the lights, but the winter path is a ski track, so be considerate.

Bus routes are in the Troms Reise app. Bring ice cleats (brodder), reflectors and a headlamp, and remember you are responsible for your own safety.

## Getting there and around

Tromsø Airport, Langnes, is on the west side of Tromsøya, and the Hurtigruten coastal ships call at the city. The Tromsø Bridge links the island to the mainland and the cable car, and the Sandnessund Bridge leads to the large island of Kvaløya. That means you can stay centrally and still reach a dark spot quickly. Without a car, buses and walking get you a long way on Tromsøya itself; to reach the fjords of Kvaløya or the inland valleys you need a rental car or a guided tour. Make sure the rental car has winter tyres, and plan your drive back before you get tired and cold.

## When it pays to drive out

The further from city lights, the more you see of faint aurora and the fine detail in outbursts. Many drive out to Kvaløya west of the city, to its fjords or out towards the coast, such as Sommarøy (about 60 km by road). Others head inland when the coast is cloudy. Skibotn on the Lyngen fjord is about 125 km away, and the E8 continues up the Skibotn valley to Kilpisjärvi on the Finnish side (about 170 km from Tromsø). These are approximate road distances; in winter the drive often takes considerably longer than summer estimates.

Driving far without a plan makes little sense. Check the cloud forecast for each layer (low, medium and high cloud) and look for gaps before you leave. Read more in [clouds and clear skies](id:clouds-weather). On the coast the weather can change fast, and a clear patch can vanish while you drive towards it.

For winter driving the basic rules are simple: drive to the conditions, never stop on the road or on a bend to take photos, and watch out for reindeer and moose. See [safety and clothing](id:safety-clothing) and the road reports from the Norwegian Public Roads Administration (Statens vegvesen) before you go.

## Etiquette: be a guest people are happy to see

Aurora tourism has grown fast, and locals notice. In February 2024 NRK reported on residents of Ersfjordbotn on Kvaløya who found tourists in their gardens, on their porches and at the turning area by their house. In May 2026 NRK reported that Tromsø municipality wants fixed routes and designated stopping places for aurora tourism, a proposal tour operators are critical of. Rules may therefore change; follow local signs and information.

Visit Tromsø's own guidelines for aurora tourism sum up the essentials:

- Do not walk into gardens or onto private property; people live there.
- Park only where it is allowed and there is space, not in passing places, on bends or where you block traffic.
- Wear reflective gear and never walk in the middle of the road. Roads are dark and winding.
- Do not light fires in rest areas or next to parked cars. If you must have a fire, keep at least 10 metres from vehicles and leave no trace.
- Let truck drivers in rest areas get their legally required rest.
- Take all litter home, and use a toilet before you head out.

## In short

- Tromsø sits under the auroral oval on most clear nights; clouds are the main limitation.
- You can see aurora from the city, but Prestvannet, Telegrafbukta and Fjellheisen are much better than the centre.
- When the coast is cloudy it may be clearer inland towards Skibotn and Finland, but check the cloud forecast first.
- Be considerate: park legally, stay out of private gardens and take your litter with you.
