Lofoten and Vesterålen may be the most photogenic places in Norway to see the aurora: peaks rising straight out of the sea, white beaches and small fishing villages as foreground. The islands lie slightly south of the core of the auroral oval, but aurora is common here from late August to mid-April, and further north in Vesterålen, on the island of Andøya, you are placed even a little better. What needs the most planning is the changeable coastal weather and the winter roads.

## How well placed are the islands?

By our calculation with the AACGM-v2 coordinate model, Svolvær lies at about 66° magnetic latitude, while Andenes at the northern tip of Andøya is at about 67°, similar to Tromsø. NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center says the equatorward edge of the auroral oval sits at around 66° magnetic latitude at Kp 0 and moves roughly 2° towards the equator for each Kp step.

For a visitor, that means:

- On very quiet nights the aurora may hang low in the north from Lofoten, while it stands higher over Andøya.
- Already at Kp 1–2, NOAA's rule of thumb puts the oval's southern edge south of the islands, so the aurora can be right overhead.
- An open view to the north helps, which is why beaches on the north side of the islands are so popular.

Read more about interpreting those numbers in [the Kp index](id:kp-index). Visit Lofoten notes that the aurora cannot be seen from mid-April to late August because the nights are too bright; see [best time of year](id:best-season).

## Coastal weather: mild, wet and quick to change

The Norwegian encyclopaedia Store norske leksikon describes Lofoten as having a coastal climate: mild winters and fairly cool summers. It is rarely as cold as inland, but precipitation and wind off the sea are part of winter here, and the weather changes fast. An evening can go from solid cloud to clear sky in a short time, and back again.

Two practical consequences:

- The cloud forecast for the next few hours matters more than the daily forecast. Watch low, medium and high cloud separately; see [clouds and clear skies](id:clouds-weather).
- The mountains create local differences. It can be clear on one side of an island and cloudy on the other, so a short drive can pay off.

In a coarse historical weather dataset (the ERA5 reanalysis), the Lofoten coast does not have fewer clear evening hours through the winter than many inland places, but its weather is less stable. Treat that as a reason to stay flexible, not as a guarantee.

## Beaches and fjords as foreground

What sets Lofoten apart from other aurora regions is the landscape. Some well-known spots (always check parking and signs locally):

- **Haukland:** A beach on the north side of Vestvågøy, about ten minutes' drive from Leknes. It has been voted Norway's most beautiful beach.
- **Uttakleiv:** Haukland's neighbour, reachable along the old road around the mountain.
- **Skagsanden:** A beach on Flakstadøya, known for the patterns in its sand and the mountains behind.
- **Ramberg:** An easily reached beach on the north side of Flakstadøya.
- **Hov on Gimsøya:** Open landscape and a beach near the golf course.
- **Bleik and Andenes on Andøya:** The far north of Vesterålen, with an open view over the sea.

Several of these beaches are on the north side of the islands, so you often get the aurora over the sea, framed by mountains on either side. Still water, wet sand and calm fjords give reflections of the aurora, but take care near the water's edge in the dark and on slippery rocks, and keep clear of tides and waves. For photo tips, see [photographing the aurora with a camera](id:camera-photo) and [photographing the aurora with a phone](id:phone-photo).

## Andøya and Vesterålen

Vesterålen lies north of Lofoten and includes the islands of Andøya, Langøya and Hadseløya. Andøya reaches furthest north and has a long history in aurora research: Andøya Space, which launches research rockets, is located at Oksebåsen between Andenes and Bleik and, according to Visit Vesterålen, has enabled scientists to study the atmosphere, the aurora and space since 1962. Its visitor centre has exhibitions and a film about the aurora, a good option on a cloudy evening.

## Winter roads

The E10 links Lofoten without ferries from Å on Moskenesøya to the E6 at Bjerkvik, according to Store norske leksikon. There are few alternative roads, so one closed tunnel or a storm can stop an aurora trip.

**Important for winter 2026–27:** The Nappstraumen tunnel (Nappstraumtunnelen) on the E10, between Vestvågøy and Flakstadøy, is being upgraded and is closed at night. According to the Norwegian Public Roads Administration (Statens vegvesen):

- Until 16 November 2026: at night (11 pm–6 am) you can only pass at 2 am.
- 16 November–6 December: closed 9:30 pm–6 am, with one night-time passage around 12:30 am.
- From 7 December and through the winter: closed 11 pm–6 am, with one passage around 12:30 am.

If you stay on the Leknes side and want to photograph on Flakstadøya, or the other way round, plan your return around the tunnel times. Times can change; check the road authority's project page before you drive.

Visit Lofoten also gives some simple winter driving advice: never stop in the middle of the road to take photos, do not blindly trust map apps that may send you onto snow-filled roads, expect much longer braking distances on ice, and wear a reflective vest if you have to get out of the car. Signs with a large "M" mark passing places (møteplass), not parking. See also [safety and clothing](id:safety-clothing).

Small planes fly to Leknes and Svolvær, the Hurtigruten coastal ships call at Stamsund and Svolvær, and the car ferry from Moskenes runs to Bodø.

## Etiquette

Many of the best spots are next to small villages where people live year-round. Visit Lofoten reminds visitors that parking is paid in many places, especially in the municipalities of Vågan, Flakstad and Moskenes, and that the signs on site apply. Use public toilets and waste stations, do not park in passing places or private driveways, and stay out of gardens and fields.

## In short

- Lofoten sits at the edge of the auroral oval on quiet nights; Andøya and Vesterålen are placed a little better.
- Coastal weather changes fast: follow the cloud forecast hour by hour and be ready to move.
- Beaches on the north side of the islands give an open view north and spectacular foregrounds.
- In winter 2026–27 the Nappstraumen tunnel on the E10 is closed at night; plan around its passage times.
